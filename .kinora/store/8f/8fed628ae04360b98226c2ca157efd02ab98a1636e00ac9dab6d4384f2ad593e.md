# Spec — the Stop hook drives the pull loop; a lease is one context

**Contract.** A worker's pull loop uses the selected agent harness's hooks and
MCP tools. The harness supplies the launch configuration and translates the
CLI's payloads and replies; the worker engine alone owns contexts, claims and
leases. The loop:

1. Session startup binds the lease to its context and gives the model the
   context's head and worker rules. Claude injects these at SessionStart; Kimi
   receives them with the launcher's kickoff at UserPromptSubmit.
2. The model calls `pull` to claim the next ready item under the bound context,
   works it, and calls `submit` with evidence.
3. Stop asks for the next ready item under the *same* context. If there is one,
   it claims it, blocks the stop and hands the claim over as the reason, so the
   session continues as a new turn with the cache warm. Stopping with an open
   claim and no submission is refused; after three refusals for one claim the
   claim is released and the lease ends as **stuck**.

**One item per turn, enforced.** After a submit, `pull` refuses for the rest of
the turn; the next item comes only with the Stop handoff. This rule is
mechanical, not a prompt.

A lease is **one context**. Its trace records an end when any of these holds:

- the bound context's queue is empty (an interactive pane remains for a human);
- **PreCompact** fires (no item is worked on a summarised transcript);
- the lease's turn budget is spent;
- a human types in the pane (UserPromptSubmit records takeover; Kimi's queued
  human turn uses its Ctrl-S steering convention);
- the agent process exits before another Stop decision.

The launcher's fixed kickoff and a CLI-generated background-task
`<task-notification>` are not takeovers. A harness whose print cleanup does not
emit SessionEnd uses an exit wrapper to settle the lease. Settlement is
idempotent, releases an open claim without pulling another, and preserves a
spent-budget or empty-queue outcome when those already hold. Beside these, the
open-claim guard ends a lease as stuck.

A claim is a write-once kino with a deadline. A crashed worker's item is
released when the deadline passes and re-pulled as attempt N+1.

**Chosen.** Stop as the loop holds the CLI open without injected input and gives
one place for handoff and the open-claim guard. Explicit process-exit settlement
covers CLIs whose final Stop or SessionEnd does not fire. One context per lease
keeps its trace one context wide.

**Rejected.** One session per item under a supervisor (a cold cache per item). A
model-driven loop without hooks. Handing over items from another context.
Treating a process exit with ready work as an empty queue.

**Escape hatch.** Headless and interactive runs share the engine and harness;
a harness may launch differently but may not create a second queue loop. Claim
heartbeat and deadline length remain separately configurable concerns.
Process exit is traced as `exit` only when work remains and no earlier reason applies.
