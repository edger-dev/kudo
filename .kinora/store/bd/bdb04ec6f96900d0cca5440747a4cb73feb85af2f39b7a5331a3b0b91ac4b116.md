# Note, 22 Oct 2026: next steps after phase 4, and where the work to dogfood comes from

Raw intent from the author, typed away from the ledger. To be designed with `kudo-design`.

## Worry

Phases 0, 2 and 4 are done, 1 and 3 nearly. The dogfood week needs real work, and most of the
author's home work right now is design and implementation, which a single phone session still
does best. Kudo's shape (long-lived contexts, many queued items, more than one lease, due dates)
fits work that recurs, waits, or spans hosts. The week needs a source of that kind of work.

## Decided by the author

- **The home lab is the first dogfood domain**: the hosts, their containers, the object-store
  bucket, credentials, flake updates, disk and backups. Health checks, "an update broke X", "is the
  access key still valid" are on-call cases and follow-ups in the author's own domain. Process control
  is what makes this save time, so it comes first.
- **Building kudo with kudo** (implement and review steps as queue items, pulled by two workers)
  is promising but waits for the next round, after contexts and the console pass basic use, to
  keep friction low now.
- **Due-dated checks** come next: an item that waits in the queue until its due time, then wakes
  a worker. The notes vault's deferred checks are the model (append-only log, a measurable pass
  condition, a fixed evidence window, closed with pass / fail / snooze and a note). A second,
  separate kudo deployment with its own local, git-backed store may use them for another domain;
  it never links to the home hub or bucket.
- **Phone access** is solved (overlay network and tunnel); the console's phone UX is iteration
  work, not a risk.
- **After process control**, the author's earlier process manager is to be retired in favour of
  the edger tools. Its console's UX is better than kudo's today; that UX crosses as notes
  (screens, interactions, what each view answers), never as code.
