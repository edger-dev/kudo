# Spec — a submit from a moved head is refused

**Contract.** Every claim records the head version current when it was taken.
A submit whose claim started from a head that has since been superseded is
**refused**, and the refusal carries the current head. The worker folds what
the other submission established into its own new head and submits again;
its claim stays live. A submit from the current head supersedes it as before.

Two submits that pass the check at the same instant still both land: the head
forks (two versions, one parent). Reading the head then shows the newest tip,
and the next submit supersedes **every** tip, so the fork closes on the next
write and neither version leaves the history.

**Chosen.** Optimistic refusal over a fork. The new head is written whole by
the worker (no per-field merge), so a fork accepted silently would drop one
worker's head lines until a human noticed. Refusing hands the merge to the one
party that understands both versions — the worker holding the work — at the
moment it has the context in hand. The residual race is kinora's own
concurrency model: forks, not locks.

**Rejected.** Accepting every fork and merging later (a cold reader sees two
heads, or the newer one only, until someone merges). One claim per context at
a time (no forks, but two workers cannot run in parallel — the tracer bullet's
second worker would idle). A lock on the head (needs a lock service the store
does not have and must stay reachable without).

**Escape hatch.** A worker that cannot merge releases its claim; the item goes
back to the queue as the next attempt, to be worked from the new head.
