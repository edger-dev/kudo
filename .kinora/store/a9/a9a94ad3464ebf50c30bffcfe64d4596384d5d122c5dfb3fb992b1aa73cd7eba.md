# Spec — a face holds no logic a second face would need

**Contract.** A face (kino://7ea24d8f08c2e3e9c4502030a86fd9d5f9e36c1067d7e2d034d2796177e719ec)
may contain **only** what is specific to presenting one engine to one consumer:
protocol adaptation, argument shaping, rendering. The moment a second face would
need a piece of logic, that logic **moves down into the engine** — it is never
copied into the second face.

This is the testable form of "a face is thin". "Thin" is a judgement; "would a
second face need this?" is a question with an answer.

**Chosen.** One rule, applied at the moment a second consumer appears. It is
cheap to obey early and expensive to retrofit: logic that has been duplicated
across two faces has also, by then, diverged.

**Rejected.** "Keep faces small" as guidance without a test — every individual
addition is small, and the boundary erodes one reasonable-looking commit at a
time. Also rejected: sharing logic between faces via a face-to-face dependency or
a shared face library; that is an engine with the wrong name, and it puts
capability where kino://d51a6329ddfc69986e2fa7ee5835e51d30c8c62c324d47e3d5c85a43ffa29801
says nothing may depend on it.

**Escape hatch.** Presentation state that exists in **no** engine and that no
second consumer wants — a console's client-side rollups, sparkline rings, sort
order — legitimately lives in its face. The test is not "does it hold state" but
"would a second face need it".

**Corollary — one parameterized tool beats N pinned ones.** Where a face exposes
a surface to an agent, near-identical duplicated entry points are a real cost:
every one is a chance to pick the wrong one. Reach across nodes with an argument,
not with another copy of the surface.
