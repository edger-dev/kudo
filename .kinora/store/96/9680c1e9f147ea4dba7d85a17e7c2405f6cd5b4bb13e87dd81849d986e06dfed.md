# A worker with nothing ready waits for the next item

**Chosen.** When the Stop hook finds nothing ready under the bound context, the
lease does not end: the hook waits, polling the store every few seconds, and
hands over the first item queued. One hook call waits at most a window (540 s,
inside the Stop hook's 600 s timeout); when the window passes with nothing, it
blocks the stop with a short "nothing yet — reply in one line and end your
turn", so the next Stop waits again. Each wait traces a `wait` event, so the
console shows the worker as waiting — not quiet — while it lives. The lease ends
(`queue-empty`) only after it has waited `KUDO_IDLE_LIMIT` seconds in a row
(default 7200) with nothing handed over; an item handed over resets the count.

**Why.** A worker started before its work was queued (or one that finished its
queue) used to end at once, so every new ask needed a new worker. Waiting keeps
one warm session per context: the cache, the repo view and the context's head
stay loaded between items.

**Rejected.** Ending and relaunching on each enqueue (a cold session per item,
and a launcher that must watch the queue). Waiting in one unbounded hook call
(the CLI kills a hook past its timeout, and a dead wait looks alive). Waking on
hub events: polling the local store is enough for now; events can shorten the
wait later without changing this contract.

**Escape hatch.** `KUDO_IDLE_LIMIT=0` ends the lease as soon as nothing is ready
(the old behavior). A human takes over during a wait as at any time: interrupt
in the pane and type; the takeover ends the lease.
